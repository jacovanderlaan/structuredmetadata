Just To Be Clear

A model is the moment an organisation says the quiet part out loud — the sentence that turns shared assumptions into something everyone can see and check.

A data model is an organisation's "just to be clear" sentence.

Everyone works on implicit assumptions — about what a customer is, when an order becomes an order, which definition of revenue counts. It works, right up until someone writes it down. Modelling is the moment you say: *just to be clear, this is what we mean.*

What actually happens in that moment

Four things, and only one of them is technical.

The tension was already there. The model does not create the contradiction; it makes it visible. Three departments already had three definitions of "active customer" — nobody had put them side by side. This is why modelling sessions feel confrontational: you are not introducing a problem, you are ending the ambiguity that was hiding it.

It is uncomfortable. Making something explicit means someone can turn out to be wrong. That is precisely why organisations prefer to stay in the register of half-sentences and reasonable assumptions. The discomfort is not a side effect of good modelling — it is the evidence that it is working.

Nothing changes except that it is now said. No system was migrated, no code written. And yet the situation is different, because it can now be checked, argued with, and built on.

Only afterwards can you go forward. As long as nobody says it, everyone keeps stacking assumptions — and pipelines get built on sand.

Why the phrase, and not "clarity"

Clarity is a virtue word: everyone claims it, nobody can be held to it. "Just to be clear" is a move you make. You can say it out loud in a meeting; you cannot say "clarity" at someone. It is a verb pretending to be a phrase.

It is also honest about the cost. In real life the sentence gets used exactly when things get tense — which is a fair description of what modelling does to a room. Compare it with Maps Everywhere, which is the same instinct applied to systems: make the implicit explicit, at every layer. *Just to Be Clear* is that principle compressed into one sentence, spoken by a person, in a room, at the uncomfortable moment.

The counter-signal

> *"Why do I feel like everyone knows something except for me?"*

That is the sound of a landscape nobody has made explicit — the new data engineer's first week, the analyst who cannot reconcile two dashboards, the executive whose numbers do not match. When you hear it, the model is missing.

Where it lives

The method layer of Breakthrough Modeling — the human moment that a model formalises.
